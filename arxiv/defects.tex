We report all three because each is easy to reproduce inadvertently, none is
visible in the resulting scores, and the third produced a published-looking
result that was entirely an artifact.

\textbf{Failed judge calls were persisted.} When a call errored the pipeline
wrote a score record of five zeros and continued, and because later runs skip
cases that already have a record, those failures became permanent. In our initial
pass this silently invalidated 231 test-split scores, 211 of them on a single
model, so that model's row was computed from 309 of 550 cases while the table
said 520. The judge now declines to persist a failed score.

\textbf{Reasoning was graded as answer.} Our model was served through vLLM
without a reasoning parser, and because Qwen chat templates pre-fill
\texttt{<think>} the model emits its reasoning, a closing \texttt{</think>} and
then its answer in one field, whereas hosted APIs return reasoning separately.
Ours was graded on roughly 4,000 characters of unfiltered reasoning prepended to
its answer while every other system was graded on the answer alone; correcting
this raised its test score by 0.36 points and moved it from third to second.
Comparisons between locally served reasoning models and hosted APIs require
checking what is actually in the string being graded.

\textbf{The judge saw inputs the model had been denied.} This occurred twice, on
two different fields. We first ran the image
ablation with the judge reading the stored prompt, which still said
\texttt{[IMAGE ATTACHED]}, while the model in the withheld arm had been told
otherwise. The models behaved correctly and said they could not describe an
embryo they had not seen; the judge, believing an image was attached, marked that
down. GPT-5.4's morphology score appeared to fall from 3.94 to 1.98, and 35 of 40
sampled justifications cited the missing description as the reason. Read at face
value it was a striking demonstration that the vision channel carries substantial
information. It was an artifact of our own prompt handling. Having fixed the
image marker we ran the two-by-two and reproduced the same mistake on the Gardner
grade: arms that withheld the grade from the model still showed it to the judge,
whose justifications then objected that a description ``conflicts with the
provided lab grade of 1DD'' about a grade the model never received. On one
representative case morphology grounding moved from 2 to 4 once the judge's copy
matched the model's.

Both were fixed in one place. The judge now derives its copy of the prompt from
the run metadata recording what the model was given. Fixing only the text would
have introduced a third asymmetry, this time in the judge, since a judge denied
the image in one arm and given it in another is a different instrument in each;
in the ablation arms the embryo is therefore still attached to the judge call
even when the model was denied it, together with an instruction that the model
could not see it and must not be penalised for declining to describe it.

All results in this paper follow all three corrections: every score is produced
by GPT-5.4 with the embryo in front of it, no score record contains a judge
failure, and in every arm the judge was told exactly what the model had been
given. The confounded score sets ship alongside the corrected ones, under
directory names identifying the confound.
